Protective Security Assessment Program

One consistent way to decide how important a secure room or area is, how exposed it is, and which protections it must have.

The rule that holds the model together

The room’s tier comes only from the business answers in LAUNCH. The tier sets a mandatory baseline. SHIELD never lowers a tier or waives a baseline. It tells the technical team which type of control fits, and shows leaders where exposure is highest.

1

LAUNCH

Location, Asset Use, Necessity, Criticality & Hierarchy

The person responsible for the room answers eight business questions. After assessor validation, the result is Tier 1–4 or Non-Tier, plus any legal or regulatory requirement.

2

SHIELD

Security Hardening & Intrusion Exposure-Level Determination

The physical security assessor scores nine observable conditions, each with written anchors and recorded evidence, so two assessors reach the same answer.

3

Baseline and selection

Tier + requirement → control type

The tier and any legal requirement set what must be in place. The technical team (STAT) picks the control type and configuration. Every baseline must be met.

For the business owner

Answer for the actual room. You are not asked to choose cameras, readers, alarms or barriers. Security validates and confirms the result.

For leaders

The same SHIELD factor data, aggregated within each tier, shows where exposure is highest and where investment will reduce it most.

How the process works

Business input and security assessment are kept separate. Each step has one owner.

Design rules

  • LAUNCH captures business impact. The assessor validates it. LAUNCH does not ask about cameras, readers, alarms or barriers.
  • SHIELD captures observable exposure only. It is not a pass/fail checklist of installed controls.
  • The tier changes only through a formal reassessment of business criticality, never because of SHIELD or installed controls.
  • Confirmed legal or regulatory requirements are added to the baseline as mandatory parameters.

Tier logic and scoring

Four fixed tiers, plus Non-Tier. The tier definitions are governing; the score supports consistent assignment.

LAUNCH weighted score PROPOSED weights and points

Score = Q3 × 0.20 + Q5 × 0.25 + Q6 × 0.20 + Q7 × 0.25 + Q8 × 0.10. Q1, Q2 and Q4 carry no weight. Every question scores 1–5 in the order the answers appear, and higher always means higher business criticality.

Why these weights PROPOSED working values

No published standard assigns percentages to these questions. Public guidance agrees on one point: consequence of loss comes first. FEMA 452 rates asset value by the impact of losing the asset, with loss of life always highest. IBM calls evaluating consequences “arguably the most important” step in asset criticality analysis. NIST SP 800-34 builds criticality from impact and tolerable downtime together without ranking them. None of them ranks recovery time against redundancy or gives numeric weights, so Q5 and Q7 carry the highest weight and the order of the others is a judgment to be calibrated in the pilot. This is a pattern across named sources, not a statistical study.

Compare the two-decimal score to the thresholds without rounding up. Q8 “Not applicable” scores 1 only after the assessor confirms nothing requires redundancy.

Non-Tier test PROPOSED

Tier 4 already covers scores 1.00–1.99, so Non-Tier needs its own test. All three must be true:

  1. The weighted score is below the governance cutoff (value not yet set).
  2. Q2 is “No”. A “Yes” or an open “Unknown” blocks Non-Tier.
  3. The assessor confirms Q3 and Q7 are at their lowest answers.

If any gate fails, the room is Tier 4.

What never changes the tier

  • SHIELD results
  • Installed controls, or controls being added or removed
  • Exposure bands or heat-map colors

Confirmed legal or regulatory requirements still apply even when the weighted result is Non-Tier.

Assessor authority PROPOSED

A tier is not final until the Physical Security Assessor confirms the LAUNCH responses. The assessor may correct a response the evidence does not support, recording a reason code, and the documented correction is final. Detail is on the Governance tab.

Read the factors first

The factor columns show what to fund. High Entry and Time scores point to hardening and detection. High Human and Ext scores point to visitor management and opening protection.

The band only sorts

The exposure band orders the list, but the weights are not yet validated. Treat the factor pattern as the primary evidence.

Baseline is separate

Baseline status has its own column and is never blended into exposure. An open item is tracked as an action, not hidden in a score.

Evidence, audit and decisions

What makes a result repeatable and auditable, how it will be tested, and what still needs a decision.

Required with every SHIELD answer PROPOSED

  1. Score (1–5) chosen from the written anchor
  2. Evidence item: photo, drawing, timed-walk record, schedule or access log, or search record
  3. Where the evidence came from
  4. Assessor name and date
  5. Version of the job aids and anchors used
  6. A note only when an answer sits between two anchors

If evidence is ambiguous: record the higher-exposure anchor and flag it for QA review. Never score from memory, anecdote, or what an installed control is expected to do.

LAUNCH validation record PROPOSED

The owner answers; the assessor confirms as Confirmed, Corrected (with a reason code) or Returned to the owner.

  • Owner’s answer and assessor-confirmed answer, per question
  • Change flag and reason code
  • Evidence reference
  • Legal requirement citation and parameters (for example a retention period)
  • Reviewer ID, date, form and scoring version
  • Result: Tier 1–4 or Non-Tier, with the requirement flag

LAUNCH validation: assessor authority PROPOSED

1

Confirmation required

A tier is not final until the Physical Security Assessor has reviewed and confirmed the LAUNCH responses.

2

Authority to correct

The assessor is authorized to correct any owner or ACO response that the evidence does not support.

3

Documented basis

Each correction is recorded with a reason code and an evidence reference.

4

Final determination

A documented correction is final. The audit record retains the original owner response and the corrected response.

Items to define before approval: reason-code list; time limit for the owner’s response to a returned item; independence of the assessor from the business unit; reassessment triggers when business conditions change.

Pilot: proving two assessors reach the same answer PROPOSED

The weights and bands are placeholders until this test is run. Sample size and the agreement target are set by governance before the pilot starts.
